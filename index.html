The Best Thing I've Read Lately: The Heroin Diaries

The best thing I've read lately is probably The Heroin Diaries by Nikki Sixx. It sounds like a gruesome book, and admittedly it is. The book is essentially a printed copy of the diary of Nikki Sixx, Mötley Crüe's infamous bassist, dated from Christmas Day 1986, when he overdosed, to a year later, Christmas Day 1987, when he started to get clean. Along with the book being a very interesting glimpse into the mind of a tortured artist, “Sikki Nikki” as he refers to himself, it also has some amazing reflections from 2007's now-sober Nikki Sixx as he reads back and reflects on his old ways and leaves some very wise author's notes along the way.

The reason why The Heroin Diaries is such an amazing book is because of how unfiltered it actually is. In our society, we see addiction as this gross, shameful thing, so we sugarcoat it, hide it, and become uncomfortable whenever the topic is brought up. But in reality, addiction is everywhere we look. We can't just look away from it and pretend it's all sunshine and rainbows. As a society we should talk about it more, not to normalize it, but to encourage those who are struggling to stop hiding and feel comfortable to come forward and seek the help they need before it's too late. Our silence and sensitivity is literally killing people.

At no point does the book exaggerate or downplay the horrors of craving or withdrawal; it says it exactly how it is. The raw truth of his words hits especially hard as someone who's been through addictions myself. Because of that, the book was almost a self-help read. Seeing how far someone like Nikki Sixx has come since the 80s, and now having been sober for 25 years, is honestly a big reason I was able to clean up my own act and better myself as a person.

This book will always have a special place in my heart for personal reasons, and I re-read it every year to remind myself why. I'm sure it's the same for many others, as it's commonly found in hundreds of rehab centers in America. For that reason, I'd recommend this book to anyone who's struggling, has struggled, or knows those who are. And if books aren't your thing, he even made an album with his band, Sixx:A.M., which tells the same story.
